## What I am working on now

### Corrigibility and the paradoxes of control

My MRes dissertation reconceptualises corrigibility to address the paradoxes of control, then stress-tests that reframing empirically. 

### Early warning signals for emergent misalignment (BASE Fellowship, October to December 2026)

Can optimisation geometry in alignment-relevant subspaces predict emergent misalignment? 

I run controlled fine-tuning experiments and compare global training metrics with metrics projected onto alignment-relevant subspaces, to test whether the projected signals change before misalignment shows up in behavioural evaluations.

### Gradual disempowerment as a control or alignment problem (Nous AIxPhilosophy Graduation Project Mentorship, October to December 2026)

Is gradual disempowerment a control failure, an alignment failure, or a third kind of failure that current safeguards miss? 

Our research team tests four mechanisms by which human oversight could erode. My part is conceptual and empirical. Conceptually, I define what it means for optimisation to narrow a person's options, adapt Turner's power-seeking metric to measure the user's options in place of the agent's, and set the baseline that narrowing is measured against. Empirically, I am building a toy recommender to test whether optimisation pressure alone produces that narrowing, with no corrigibility or deference failure involved, and what this implies for where the mechanism sits in the control and alignment taxonomy.
